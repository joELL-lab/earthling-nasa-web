ESTABLISHED JULY 29, 1958

About NASA — For the Benefit of All

For nearly seven decades, NASA has united scientists, engineers, astronauts, and global partners to unlock the secrets of Earth, our solar system, and the deep universe.

VISION & GUIDING PRINCIPLES

Mission & Core Values

Vision: Exploring the secrets of the universe for the benefit of all.  •  Mission: NASA explores the unknown in air and space, innovates for the benefit of humanity, and inspires the world through discovery.

Safety

Unwavering commitment to crew, spacecraft, and public safety across every countdown and mission phase.

Integrity

Open scientific transparency, rigorous peer review, and accountability in every discovery we publish.

Teamwork

Collaborating across NASA centers, international space agencies, universities, and industry partners.

Excellence

Pushing precision engineering to land rovers across interplanetary distances and operate at L2.

Inclusion

Bringing diverse perspectives and global STEM talent together so spaceflight reflects all of humanity.

CHRONOLOGY OF EXPLORATION

Decades of Cosmic Milestones

1958

NASA Founded

The National Aeronautics and Space Act takes effect, uniting aeronautical research laboratories to launch America’s civilian space program.

1969

Apollo 11 — First Steps on the Moon

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin land the Eagle lunar module at Tranquility Base while Michael Collins orbits above, fulfilling humanity’s lunar voyage.

1981

Space Shuttle Era Begins

Space Shuttle Columbia launches on STS-1, introducing reusable winged spacecraft that deployed satellites, Spacelab, and the Hubble Space Telescope.

1990

Hubble Space Telescope Deployed

Placed above Earth’s turbulent atmosphere, Hubble transforms astronomy—dating the cosmos and photographing galaxies near the edge of observable time.

1998

International Space Station Assembly

Global partners join Zarya and Unity modules in low-Earth orbit, establishing a continuous human presence in space since November 2000.

2021

James Webb Space Telescope Launch

Unfolding a 6.5-meter gold-coated infrared mirror at Sun–Earth L2, Webb captures cosmic dawn, stellar nurseries, and distant exoplanets.

ARTEMIS ERA

Return to the Moon & Path to Mars

Powered by SLS and Orion, Artemis establishes sustainable lunar exploration at the Moon’s south pole as a proving ground for human missions to Mars.

OPERATIONS NETWORK

NASA Field Centers

Merritt Island, Florida

Kennedy Space Center

NASA’s premier multi-user spaceport—launching Artemis SLS rockets, crewed commercial flights, and deep-space planetary probes.

Houston, Texas

Johnson Space Center

Home to Mission Control Center and the NASA Astronaut Corps, leading human spaceflight operations, Orion, and ISS expeditions.

Pasadena, California

Jet Propulsion Laboratory

Lead center for robotic solar system exploration—building Perseverance, Europa Clipper, Juno, Voyager, and operating the Deep Space Network.

Greenbelt, Maryland

Goddard Space Flight Center

Hub for Earth science satellites, heliophysics, and flagship space observatories including Hubble and the James Webb Space Telescope.

PEOPLE BEHIND THE MISSIONS

Modern Figures & Pioneers

Langley Research Center • Orbital Mechanics

Katherine Johnson

Pioneering NASA mathematician whose orbital trajectory calculations proved vital to Mercury, Apollo 11 lunar rendezvous, and the Space Shuttle.

Artemis II Mission Specialist • Astronaut

Christina Koch

Holder of the longest single spaceflight by a woman (328 days) and participant in the first all-female spacewalk, now assigned to fly around the Moon on Artemis II.

Mission Control & Robotics Teams

Next-Gen Mission Engineers

Thousands of flight controllers, software architects, and planetary scientists designing autonomous Mars navigation and deep-space optical downlinks.
